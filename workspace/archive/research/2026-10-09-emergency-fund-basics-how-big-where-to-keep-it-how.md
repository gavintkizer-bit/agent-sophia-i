# Emergency Fund Basics: How Big, Where to Keep It, How to Build Stress-Free

## Question
How much emergency savings does a gig worker with variable 1099 income need, where should it live, and what's the most stress-free way to build it? This matters because the owner earns through DoorDash and Uber Eats (no steady paycheck), has no rent or phone bill but can get overwhelmed, and wants practical, low-friction ideas.

## Answer

- **Target size: 4–6 months of baseline expenses.** Gig workers need more than the 3–6 months recommended for salaried employees because "Income can slow down outside busy seasons" and a slow season can arrive without warning. Start with a $1,000 mini-fund to prevent small emergencies from becoming debt. [https://indeedflex.com/career-hub/financial-tips/emergency-fund-guide, read 2026-10-09] **verified** ("Income can slow down outside busy seasons")

- **Calculate "baseline" as rent, utilities, food, insurance, debt payments, transportation only.** Exclude discretionary spending. With no rent or phone bill, the owner's baseline is smaller than most—a real advantage. Multiply that monthly number by 4–6 to find the target. [https://indeedflex.com/career-hub/financial-tips/emergency-fund-guide, read 2026-10-09] **unverified**

- **Where to keep it: a high-yield savings account at a different bank than everyday checking.** These "offer easy access to your money while allowing your balance to earn interest," earning 4–5% APY and staying accessible in 1–2 business days—friction enough to prevent impulse withdrawals, close enough for a real emergency. Don't let it sit in checking (earning 0.01%). [https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/where-to-keep-emergency-fund/, read 2026-10-09] **verified** ("offer easy access to your money while allowing your balance to earn interest")

- **Build stress-free: start with $5–10 per week**—in fact, "$10 a week removes the psychological weight of "saving for an emergency" and replaces it with a simple, repeatable habit." Automate a small transfer from checking to savings on payday so you never "decide" to save. Capture windfalls (tax refunds, rebates, bonus earnings) by directing them straight to the fund. Set achievable milestones ($500, then $1,000) before the big target. [https://www.theprosperitypeople.com/blog/2026/9/21/low-stress-ways-to-create-an-emergency-fund, read 2026-10-09] **verified** ("$10 a week removes the psychological weight of "saving for an emergency" and replaces it with a simple, repeatable habit")

- **Name the account something clear** (e.g., "Emergency" or "Rainy Day") to keep the purpose front of mind and discourage non-emergency spending. It's a small psychological anchor. [https://www.theprosperitypeople.com/blog/2026/9/21/low-stress-ways-to-create-an-emergency-fund, read 2026-10-09] **unverified**
