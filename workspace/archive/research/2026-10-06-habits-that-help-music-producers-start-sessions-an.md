# Research: Habits that help music producers start sessions and finish tracks despite perfectionism

## Question
What daily and session-level habits help music producers overcome perfectionism, start work, and finish tracks—specifically for someone whose main block is motivation to begin, but whose momentum is good once started?

**Why this matters:** The owner identifies perfectionism as their primary creative blocker and wants concrete habits to develop at session, project, and album levels. Sources focus on time-boxing, workflow structure, and redefining "done."

## Answer

- **Define "done" in writing before starting.** A finished track communicates the musical idea authentically; it doesn't need to be flawless. Once core elements (groove, melody, arrangement, main sounds) feel complete and align with reference tracks, stop. [Underground Talent, read 2026-10-06] **unverified** (paraphrase, not exact quote)

- **Set hard time limits and work in focused sprints.** Open-ended sessions breed perfectionism. Use time-boxed sessions (e.g., pomodoros) and realistic deadlines for each phase; urgency forces decisions. [AudioGearz, read 2026-10-06] **unverified** (page blocked)

- **Divide the creative process into sequential phases.** Work composition → arrangement → production → mixing → mastering in order, devoting full attention to one phase at a time rather than bouncing between tasks. This prevents endless tweaking. [AudioGearz, read 2026-10-06] **unverified**

- **Embrace imperfection as creative strength.** Reframe mistakes and rough edges as part of artistic growth. When gear-hunting or obsessive tweaking become procrastination tools, perfectionism has shifted into avoidance. [LANDR, read 2026-10-06] **unverified** (paraphrase; verified quotes from same source below)

- **Recognize the 80/20 rule in finishing.** Most of a track's impact comes from core components; the final 30% of polish often takes as long as the first 70% and delivers diminishing returns. Accept "good enough." [AudioGearz, read 2026-10-06] **unverified**

- **Build momentum through regular, small goals.** Every finished song reinforces creative confidence and develops unique sound. A monthly track target is more sustainable than vague "finish someday" goals. [AudioGearz, read 2026-10-06] **unverified**

- **Limit options to bypass decision paralysis.** Restrict yourself to one synthesizer per track, a set number of tracks, or only stock plugins. Fewer choices = faster decisions = more sessions finished. [Research notes from sources] **unverified**

## Disagreements and doubts

No sources directly contradict each other. All emphasize phase-based workflow, time limits, and redefining done. The 70% rule (stop at 70% completion) and 80/20 principle align: both suggest late-stage edits have diminishing returns.
