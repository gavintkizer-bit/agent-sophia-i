# Spotting money scams and protecting financial privacy online

**Question:** What are the easiest, lowest-stress ways for a young gig worker (1099 income, multiple jobs, moving states) to spot scams, protect banking information, and keep financial records safe without getting overwhelmed?

**Why it matters:** The owner earns gig income from delivery work (DoorDash, Uber Eats), is about to move to Colorado for a seasonal job, banks online, and needs practical protections that don't add anxiety. Gig workers face unique risks: multiple income sources, online banking, frequent account changes with relocation, and social-media-based recruitment scams.

---

## Answer

**Scam spotting (the 3 red flags to watch for):**
- **Pressure to move fast or secrecy:** Legitimate platforms and people don't rush you or ask you to switch to private chats, hidden payments (Zelle, wire transfers, gift cards, crypto), or new apps. Take time; pause before money changes hands. [Chase.com, read 2026-10-11, **verified**: "nearly 50% of scams reported to Chase originate on social media"]
- **Impersonation of banks or apps:** Your bank will never ask you to move money to "fix fraud"—that's a scammer tactic. If you get a message about your account, go directly to the official app or website (don't click links) and check yourself. [Chase.com, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**]
- **Too-good-to-be-true job offers:** Gig workers get targeted with "high-paying work" that asks for personal info first or promises cash with little work. Stick to your existing apps (DoorDash, Uber Eats, your seasonal employer). [Chase.com, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**]

**Separation and record-keeping (low-effort, high-payoff):**
- **Separate bank account for gig income:** One account for delivery earnings, one for personal money. This is the single hardest-working protection: it keeps tax records clear, makes deductions visible, and protects your personal account if a platform or tax issue arises. When you move to Colorado, just open a Colorado account and transfer over time. [IRS gig economy guidance, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**; TaxAct blog, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**]
- **Keep 1099s and platform statements:** Save every 1099-K and 1099-NEC form you receive, plus 3 years of bank statements for your business account. You don't need to organize receipts perfectly—just save them. The 1099 forms come in early 2027 and show what you earned; they matter for taxes. [IRS, read 2026-10-11, **verified**: "From part-time, temporary or side work"]
- **Report all gig income, even if no 1099 arrives:** You must report every dollar you earned on your taxes, whether DoorDash sent a form or not. (The $20,000/200-transaction threshold for 1099-K only means DoorDash didn't send the form—not that you don't owe tax on those earnings.) [IRS gig economy guidance, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**]

**Moving and privacy (when you relocate to Colorado):**
- **Bank branch changes are normal:** When you move states, your bank account stays the same; no action needed. Your DoorDash and Uber Eats accounts follow you. Tell them your new address when you're settled so they know where to send tax forms. [TurboTax gig worker guide, read 2026-10-11, **unverified**]
