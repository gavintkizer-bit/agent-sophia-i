# Research: Round-up and micro-saving tools (Bank of America's Keep the Change)

## Question
Do round-up savings programs (like Bank of America's Keep the Change) actually help users save, and are they suitable for a gig worker with irregular income who needs low-effort, low-stress money management?

**Why it matters:** The owner works gig economy jobs (DoorDash and Uber Eats, 1099 income) and gets overwhelmed easily. Understanding whether an automated savings tool would help him build emergency funds without adding stress is relevant to the money research routine and calm financial planning.

## Answer

1. **Enrollment and retention are strong**: "99% of those who sign up stay in the program" (Savings Project), suggesting users find it genuinely useful once activated. The program attracted 2 million customers in its first year and over 12 million total, collectively saving $2 billion. [savingsproject.org, read 2026-10-10] **verified**

2. **Amounts accumulate meaningfully over time**: "Saving at this seemingly small rate, through simple and automatic rounding of everyday purchases, can allow an individual to put away $400 for an emergency in less than a year." Individual rounds are tiny ($0.01–$0.99), but they compound. [savingsproject.org, read 2026-10-10] **verified**

3. **No account fees for the service itself**, but the accounts it requires may carry monthly fees ($10 if balance or deposit thresholds aren't met). It's important to check your specific Bank of America account tier. [mybanktracker.com, read 2026-10-10] **unverified**

4. **It is passive once set up**: After enrollment, the user "essentially forgets" the money is being saved, reducing cognitive load—a key requirement for someone who gets overwhelmed. [savingsproject.org, read 2026-10-10] **unverified**

5. **Effectiveness depends on spending frequency**: Round-up apps work only on debit card transactions; if you spend infrequently or use cash, savings won't accumulate meaningfully. Gig workers with irregular income may have weeks of low spending. [Medium/moneycrashers.com, read 2026-10-10] **unverified**

6. **Opportunity cost**: Using Keep the Change means forgoing rewards cash back (1–5%) on the same purchases if charged to a rewards credit card instead. This may outweigh the savings psychology benefit for frequent spenders. [mybanktracker.com, read 2026-10-10] **unverified**

## Disagreements and doubts

- **Program claims vs. research reality**: Bank of America cites the $2 billion saved figure, but does not widely publicize average per-user savings (likely $100–$500/year based on $400/year in the Savings Project source). The marketing suggests larger totals than individuals typically see.
- **Low-income claims**: Sources suggest round-up apps work for low-income savers, but most research assumes digital-heavy spending. A gig worker who uses cash for some purchases may see reduced results.
- **No independent study of Keep the Change specifically**: I found no recent peer-reviewed research evaluating Keep the Change itself; sources are marketing, blog reviews, and design case studies. The Savings Project article is the most analytical, but isn't a randomized trial.

## Bottom line for the owner

**Keep the Change is low-stress and easy (setup and forget), but its help is modest and context-dependent.** 

- **If the owner uses his debit card frequently for meals and small purchases**, round-ups could accumulate $200–$400/year toward an emergency fund with zero additional effort—appealing given his overwhelm sensitivity.
- **If he uses cash for tips or multiple payment methods**, the benefit shrinks significantly.
- **The real advantage is behavioral**: it automates the hardest part of saving (remembering to transfer money). For someone who struggles with starts, automation is powerful.
- **The real catch**: account fees could eat the entire savings if he's on a account tier with maintenance costs. He should verify his specific BofA account terms before enabling it.
- **Better alternative to consider**: automatic recurring transfer ($10–$20/week) from checking to a high-yield savings account (currently 4–5% APY) would earn interest and give full control over the amount. This works whether he spends digitally or in cash. Both require one setup; the transfer is equally passive.
