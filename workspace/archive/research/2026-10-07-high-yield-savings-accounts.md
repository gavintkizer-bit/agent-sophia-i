# High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSA)

**Date:** 2026-10-07

## Question
How do high-yield savings accounts work, what rates are available this month, and how is the money protected? (For the owner: gig income (1099), no rent/phone bill, wants to save more; needs low-effort, low-stress options.)

## Answer

- **What it is:** A savings account at an online bank that earns 4–5% annual interest (called APY), about 10 times higher than traditional savings (0.37%). Money stays safe and accessible whenever needed. [NerdWallet, read 2026-10-07, unverified]

- **Current rates (Oct 2026):** Best available are 4.25–4.27% APY with no fees or minimums. Axos Bank offers a temporary promotional 5% for six months if you have a linked checking account. Rates vary by bank and change over time. [Yahoo Finance (10-06-2026), verified: "Earn up to 4.25% APY"; Peak Bank 4.27% per The College Investor, unverified]

- **FDIC protection:** Deposits are insured up to $250,000 per account per person if the bank fails. Your money doesn't move; your balance simply sits there earning interest. [Openbank, read 2026-10-07, unverified (the checker did not confirm the quote; it matches the standard FDIC limit, Sophia): "coverage of up to $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category against bank failure"]

- **No effort, no fees:** Online banks have low costs so they pass high rates to you. No monthly fees, no minimum balance (usually $0–$100), no need to visit a branch. Transfers take 1–3 business days. [NerdWallet, Forbes, read 2026-10-07, unverified]

- **For gig income:** Some HYSAs offer multiple "buckets" (labeled subaccounts) to separate tax reserves, emergency fund, and savings in one account. Examples: Ally (up to 30 buckets), Marcus by Goldman Sachs. This can make quarterly tax planning simpler. [Keeper Tax, read 2026-10-07, unverified]

- **Watch:** Rates are *variable*—they can drop if interest rates fall. Also, some promotional rates expire (e.g., Axos 5% ends after six months). Read the fine print before opening. [Forbes, NerdWallet, unverified]

- **Gig-worker advantage:** No salary verification or employer direct deposit required (unlike some checking accounts). 1099 income works fine. [Keeper Tax search results, unverified]

## Disagreements and doubts

- **Exact current top rate:** Yahoo Finance and The College Investor both cite 4.25–4.27% as the standard best rate (not promotional). NerdWallet's 5% at Axos is labeled promotional/temporary with conditions (linked account, $1,500 daily balance). I couldn't confirm whether the 5% requires direct deposit; Keeper Tax mentions deposit requirements but didn't specify.
- **Rates by bank:** The College Investor, Forbes, NerdWallet, and Keeper Tax list different banks as top (Peak, CIT, SoFi, Axos, Marcus). All are in the 4–4.27% range. Since rates change daily, this is expected; pick any of these and the difference is small.
- **Withdrawal limits:** Traditional HYSAs allow unlimited transfers, but I didn't verify this in the fetched sources. Openbank mentioned "withdrawal limits" may apply but didn't specify.
