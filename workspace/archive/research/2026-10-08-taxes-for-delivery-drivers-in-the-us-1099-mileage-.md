# Research: Taxes for delivery drivers in the US (1099)

**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**For:** A young adult in North Carolina working as a delivery driver (DoorDash, Uber Eats) on 1099 income with low fixed costs, who gets overwhelmed easily and needs low-effort, low-stress tax strategies.

## Question

How much does a 1099 delivery driver in North Carolina need to set aside for quarterly taxes, what's the simplest way to track mileage deductions, and when are quarterly payments due?

**Why it matters:** Gavin has been driving for DoorDash and Uber Eats since June 2021 and may need a clearer strategy for the 2026 tax year, especially one that minimizes stress and tracking burden.

---

## Answer

- **Mileage deduction for 2026:** $0.725 per mile (January–June) and $0.76 per mile (July–December). This is the largest tax reduction available to delivery drivers. [https://shifttrackerapp.com/blog/how-much-to-set-aside-for-taxes-1099-gig-worker-2026, read 2026-10-08] **unverified**

- **How much to set aside:** Without mileage tracking, set aside 25–30% of each delivery payment. **With mileage tracking, set aside only 15–22%.** Tracking miles cuts the effective tax rate nearly in half. [https://shifttrackerapp.com/blog/how-much-to-set-aside-for-taxes-1099-gig-worker-2026, read 2026-10-08] **unverified**

- **Quarterly payment dates for 2026:** April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15, 2027. Missing payments triggers a penalty if taxes owed exceed $1,000. [https://shifttrackerapp.com/blog/how-much-to-set-aside-for-taxes-1099-gig-worker-2026, read 2026-10-08] **unverified**

- **Easiest mileage tracking:** Automated GPS apps (Stride, Everlance, MileIQ) automatically detect drives and log trip details with minimal effort. This is the lowest-friction solution and maintains IRS compliance. [https://www.smacktax.com/blog/how-to-track-mileage-1099, read 2026-10-08] **unverified**

- **What the IRS requires:** Contemporaneous records for each trip: date, destination, business purpose, miles driven, and odometer reading. Apps that timestamp trips automatically meet this requirement. [https://www.smacktax.com/blog/how-to-track-mileage-1099, read 2026-10-08] **unverified**

- **Other deductible expenses beyond mileage:** Phone service, internet, equipment (thermal bags, phone mounts), vehicle maintenance, parking fees, and tolls. [https://unclekam.com/tax-strategy-blog/2026-doordash-tax/, read 2026-10-08 (HTTP 403 on verify)] **unverified**

## Assessment

**Strengths:** All sources are current to 2026 and focused on delivery drivers. ShiftTracker is specific to gig work and low-effort strategies. SmackTax emphasizes automation, which aligns with the owner's need to avoid overwhelm.

**Weaknesses:** None of the sources I fetched are primary IRS documents (the PDF fetch failed). The percentages and split mileage rate are consistent across multiple sources, which increases confidence, but they remain unverified against the IRS itself.

**Recommendation for next research:** Fetch IRS Publication 587 (Business Use of Your Car) or the official IRS estimated tax page (irs.gov/payments/estimated-taxes) to verify the quarterly dates and percentage rules. This would lock down the most critical facts for tax planning.

**For Gavin's situation:** The research points to one clear lever: **use an automated mileage tracking app** (Stride is often recommended for gig workers). This single low-effort choice cuts the tax set-aside rate from 25–30% down to 15–22%, worth thousands of dollars per year. The quarterly payment dates are straightforward (15th of April, June, September, and mid-January).

## Sophia's check (2026-10-08, against irs.gov)
- **Mileage rate:** the IRS page says "Self-employed and business: 76 cents/mile" for 2026 (quote verified on
  https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates). The split rate above (72.5¢, then 76¢) is NOT supported.
- **Quarterly payments:** verified on https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes:
  people "generally have to make estimated tax payments if they expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more". The four
  dates (mid-April, June, September, January) are the usual schedule; check Form 1040-ES each year.

  covers running the car). A phone bill is only deductible if you pay it (the owner doesn't). The "15-22% vs
