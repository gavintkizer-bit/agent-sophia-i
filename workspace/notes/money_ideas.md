# Money ideas (vetted by Sophia)

Calm, concrete ideas for saving or earning more, from Cal's research twice a day (see
`money_research_queue.md`). Sophia keeps an idea only after checking its source. Each idea: what
it is, why it fits the owner (no rent or phone bill, ~$143/month fixed, low-stress), the first small
step, and the source.

## 1. Keep savings in a high-yield savings account (2026-10-07)
- **What:** an online savings account, federally insured (FDIC, up to $250,000), that pays far more interest
  than a regular savings account: about 4.25% a year at the top this month, against well under 1% at most banks.
- **Why it fits:** with no rent or phone bill, whatever you save just sits there. Here it grows with no effort.
  Watch for: rates change over time, and the highest "promo" rates (like 5%) last only a few months.
- **First small step:** none to take alone. If you want this, we pick one together at a meeting (I never open
  accounts or move money for you).
- **Source:** Yahoo Finance rate snapshot, 2026-10-06 (quote checked: "Earn up to 4.25% APY");
  archive/research/2026-10-07-high-yield-savings-accounts.md.

## 2. Let an app count your delivery miles (2026-10-08)
- **What:** every business mile you drive lowers your taxable income: **76 cents a mile in 2026** (IRS). Miles
  for both DoorDash and Uber Eats count. The IRS wants a record made at the time, which is why a mileage app
  that logs drives automatically is the low-effort way.
- **Why it fits:** for a driver this is usually the biggest deduction, and once it's set up it runs by itself.
- **First small step:** none to do alone. At a meeting we look at one or two free mileage apps together, and
  you pick (or don't).
- **Source:** irs.gov standard mileage rates (quote checked: "Self-employed and business: 76 cents/mile");
  archive/research/2026-10-08-taxes-for-delivery-drivers-in-the-us-1099-mileage-.md.

## 3. Put tax money aside from each payout (2026-10-08)
- **What:** gig apps don't hold back taxes. The IRS expects quarterly payments from people who will owe
  $1,000 or more, four times a year (mid-April, June, September, January).
- **Why it fits:** a small, automatic set-aside avoids a scary bill later. It pairs with idea 1: the money
  can wait in a savings account and earn interest until it's due.
- **First small step:** none alone. Working out the right share is a job for a free tax helper (IRS VITA
  sites; that's queue topic 13). I'll bring it to a meeting once that topic is researched.
- **Source:** irs.gov estimated taxes (quote checked: "expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more").

## 4. Pay yourself first, automatically, every payout (2026-10-08)
- **What:** each time a payout lands, a small share (sources suggest 5-10%) moves to savings by itself, before you
  ever see it. It's triggered by deposits, not dates, which suits gig pay.
- **Why it fits:** no willpower, no remembering, and no rent or phone bill competing for it. Even $5-10 a week is
  $260-520 a year. It's the engine that fills ideas 1 and 3.
- **First small step:** none alone. At a meeting we look at whether your bank can move money automatically when a
  deposit arrives, and you choose the share.
- **Source:** Giggle Finance (quote checked: "Set up automatic transfers to a savings account each time you receive
  a deposit"); Sunflower Bank (quote checked: "even if it's just $5 or $10 per paycheck").

## 5. A small emergency cushion, built in steps (2026-10-09)
- **What:** a separate savings pot just for surprises (a car repair, a slow week). Common guidance for gig workers
  is a bigger cushion than salaried people need, because pay can dip without warning. Milestones keep it calm:
  $500 first, then $1,000, then a few months of your basic costs.
- **Why it fits:** with no rent or phone bill, your basic costs are low, so "a few months" is a smaller, reachable
  number. Ideas 1 and 4 fill it automatically; this just gives it a name and a finish line.
- **First small step:** none alone. At a meeting we add up your basic monthly costs (gas, car insurance, food,
  the 3 keepers) once, and that number becomes the target.
- **Source:** Indeed Flex (quote checked: "Income can slow down outside busy seasons"); Capital One (quote checked:
  high-yield savings "offer easy access to your money while allowing your balance to earn interest"). The month
  counts are common guidance, not verified.

## 6. Two free guards against bank fees (2026-10-09)
- **What:** (a) a low-balance alert from your bank by text, so you hear before a payment bounces; (b) know your
  bank's rules: Bank of America stopped charging fees for returned (NSF) payments in 2022 and cut overdraft fees
  to $10. A "retry payment" is when a company tries a failed charge again a few days later.
- **Why it fits:** no new bank, no new app: one setting, once. It also guards the old-card subscriptions on your
  cut list: a card that expired isn't the same as cancelled, so those companies may keep retrying until you cancel.
- **First small step:** none alone. At a meeting, if you like, we find the alert setting in your bank app together.
- **Source:** Bankrate (quote checked: "In 2022, Bank of America stopped charging NSF fees and reduced overdraft
  fees to $10 from $35"); FDIC (quote checked: "Ask if your bank offers low balance alerts to notify you").
  Re-check the bank's own terms before relying on them.
